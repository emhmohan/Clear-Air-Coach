    Data and method

    What is real, what is estimated, and where it comes from.

    Live data

    • Air quality (PM2.5, PM10, NO₂, O₃, SO₂, CO, US AQI, hourly forecast): Open-Meteo Air Quality API, built on Copernicus CAMS atmospheric model analyses and forecasts. It is a model, not a street-level sensor, so local readings can differ.
    • Weather and mixing height (temperature, humidity, wind, boundary-layer height): Open-Meteo Forecast API. Low mixing height with calm wind marks a winter inversion, so smog is trapped.
    • Places: Open-Meteo Geocoding API.

    Standards

    • Categories use the US EPA PM2.5 breakpoints (2024 update). Multiples compare to WHO 2021 24-hour guidelines: PM2.5 15, PM10 45, NO₂ 25, SO₂ 40 µg/m³.
    • "Cigarette equivalents" use the Berkeley Earth rule of thumb (22 µg/m³ over 24 hours ≈ 1 cigarette). It is a communication aid, not a medical measure.

    Estimates (editable in the code)

    • Indoor PM2.5 factors (80% windows open, 45% shut, 15% with purifier) are typical infiltration ranges from the literature, not measurements of your home.
    • Mask benefit assumes a fitted N95/FFP2/KN95 (≥94% filter efficiency, less with leakage).
    • CO₂ factors: car 0.17, motorbike 0.10, bus 0.09, metro 0.04 kg per passenger-km (typical published averages).
    • Corsi-Rosenthal box: independent university tests show clean-air delivery comparable to commercial HEPA units.
    • The what-if simulator is an illustrative dose model (concentration × hours × breathing rate).

    AI

    Groq-hosted language models write explanations, translations, photo analysis and letters. They only receive the verified numbers above and are told not to invent figures. Always check important advice with a health professional. This tool is not medical advice.

    Privacy

    Your profile, tracker and settings stay in your browser. The server forwards your city coordinates to Open-Meteo, and your profile and questions to Groq to generate answers. Nothing is stored on the server.

    Look after each other

    • Check in on older neighbours and anyone with lung or heart conditions, especially on red-level days.
    • Open a clean-air room: a community hall, library or school classroom with a purifier running.
    • Lend a hand with masks, DIY purifier builds and weather-stripping for homes with limited means.
    • Spread the word in parent and resident groups using the alert above.

    Cut emissions together

    • School-gate no-idling pledge: engines off after 30 seconds.
    • Carpool or "walking bus" on better-air days to lower traffic at school and work.
    • No open burning of leaves, trash or crop residue. Report it to local authorities.
    • Clean-heating swaps: group buys of insulation, heat pumps or clean stoves can cut costs.

    When to get medical help

    This tool gives general guidance, not medical advice. Call your local emergency number for severe breathing difficulty.
